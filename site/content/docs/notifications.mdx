## Desktop notifications need the app bundle

On macOS, system notifications work only when Parla runs from an app bundle.
`mise run run` and `mise run bundle` create one. If you run the plain binary,
Parla stays silent. See [Install](/parla/docs/install/).

## When Parla notifies

Only live messages notify. Catching up after a disconnection and loading old
history never do.

Parla does not notify when:

- Desktop notifications are set to **Nothing**.
- The message is yours.
- The window is active and that conversation is open.
- The current time is inside your quiet hours.

Otherwise **Desktop notifications** in Settings, Notifications decides:

| Setting                        | Notifies for                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **All messages**               | Every message in conversations you are a member of, except thread replies that do not mention you |
| **Mentions and DMs** (default) | Direct messages, mentions of you, and messages containing one of your keywords                    |
| **Nothing**                    | Nothing; badges still update                                                                      |

Keywords match whole words and ignore case. A muted conversation notifies only
when someone mentions you directly.

**Show message previews** can be turned off. A notification then shows only who
wrote and where, with no message text and no file count.

Quiet hours use local `HH:MM` times and may cross midnight. You can choose the
days a quiet window starts on. Mentions still update badges during quiet hours.

## Pause notifications for a workspace

Right-click a workspace on the rail and choose **Pause notifications**, or press
`cmd-shift-d` (`ctrl-shift-d` on Linux and Windows) for the workspace you are
in. Choose 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, until tomorrow at 09:00, or a custom
length.

While paused, nothing notifies in that workspace, the rail shows a pause mark
and the sidebar says when the pause ends with **Resume notifications**. The
pause is the service's own do-not-disturb, so it follows you to other devices
when the service supports it.

On macOS, the bell in the titlebar opens the same menu.

## Per-conversation levels

Right-click a conversation and choose **Notifications**, or use the
notifications button in its header, to set that conversation to **Default**,
**All messages**, **Mentions** or **Nothing**. You can also mute it.

The header menu also has:

- **Replies to threads I follow**, on by default. A reply in a thread you started
  or took part in notifies, whatever the level except **Nothing**.
- **Sound**, on by default. Turn it off to make this conversation's
  notifications silent.
- **Mute for 1 hour**, which ends by itself, also after a restart.
- **All notification settings...**, which opens Settings, Notifications.

Parla keeps these levels on this computer when the service cannot store them for
you.

## What a notification shows

A direct message shows the sender as the title. A channel message shows "name in
#channel". The body is the message text, cut at 200 characters, or the number of
files for a message with only files.

There is at most one notification per conversation: a newer one replaces the
older one.

Click a notification to bring Parla forward and open its workspace,
conversation and, for a thread reply, that thread. Notifications disappear once
you have read the conversation.

A message notification has two buttons:

- **Reply** opens the conversation or thread so you can answer there.
- **Mark as read** marks it read without opening Parla.

Reminders and missed huddles have no buttons.

## Dock badge and menu-bar icon

The Dock badge shows the total number of mentions across all signed-in
workspaces and is hidden at zero.

Parla puts its own icon in the menu bar. It follows the light or dark menu bar
and shows a dot and the number of mentions when something needs you. Its menu
shows unread and mention counts, lists every signed-in workspace, and offers to
pause notifications, set your status, open Settings, open Parla and quit.

While the menu-bar icon is on, closing the window hides Parla and keeps it
running, so notifications and counts keep arriving. Choose **Open Parla** from
the icon to show the window again. Set `"tray": false` in `settings.json` to
remove the icon and quit when the last window closes.

On macOS, **Dock and menu bar** in Settings, General chooses whether the Dock
icon is always shown, shown only while a window is open, or never shown. The
last two need the menu-bar icon.

With **Open Parla at login** on, Parla starts at login. With the menu-bar icon
on, it starts hidden and you open it from the icon; with the icon off, it opens
its window.
